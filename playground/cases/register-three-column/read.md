14 CCR Part 214

Small Craft Mooring Permits in
Designated Harbor Areas

AGENCY: Department of Coastal Resources
(DCR).
ACTION: Final rule.
SUMMARY: The Department is revising its
regulations for permits to moor small craft in
designated harbor areas. This rule sets a single
maximum mooring length for each harbor class,
requires annual inspection of ground tackle, and
replaces the paper waiting list with an electronic
list administered by each harbor office. The
changes respond to comments received on the
proposed rule and to a rise in mooring failures
during winter storms.
DATES: This rule is effective January 4, 2027.
Permits issued before that date remain valid until
their stated expiration.
FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT:
Delia Fairweather, Office of Harbor
Management, Department of Coastal Resources,
telephone

555–0142, email

harbors@dcr.example.gov.
SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:
I. Background
Part 214 governs the placement of private
moorings in the forty-one harbor areas
designated under section 12 of the Harbors Act.
Since the rule was last revised in 2009, the
number of permitted moorings has grown by
roughly a third, while the area available for
mooring fields has not changed. Harbor masters
have reported that boats longer than the swing
radius of their mooring now routinely strike
neighbouring vessels at low water.
On March 12, 2026, the Department published
a proposed rule (51 CR 10422) that would have
set maximum mooring lengths by harbor,
required inspection of ground tackle every two
years, and moved the waiting lists online. The
comment period closed on May 11, 2026.
II. Discussion of Comments
The Department received 236 comments from
individual boat owners, yacht clubs, two
municipal harbor committees and one marine
insurer. The principal issues raised are discussed
below.
1. Maximum length. Many commenters
objected that a separate limit for each harbor
would be hard to follow for owners who move
between harbors during the season. The
Department agrees and has instead adopted a
single limit for each of the three harbor classes in
§ 214.12(b).
2. Inspection interval. The insurer and both
harbor committees urged annual rather than
biennial inspection, citing the winter storms of
2024 and 2025, in which most failed moorings
had not been inspected for more than eighteen
months. The final rule requires annual

inspection.
3. Waiting lists. Several commenters asked
that applicants already on a paper list keep their
place. The final rule carries existing places over
to the electronic list in the order of the original
application date.
III. Regulatory Analyses
This rule is not a significant regulatory action.
The Department estimates that annual inspection
will cost a permit holder between $90 and $160 a
year, depending on depth, and that the electronic
list will save harbor offices about 1,100 staff
hours a year. The rule imposes no new reporting
burden beyond the inspection certificate
described in § 214.12(c).
For the reasons stated in the preamble, the
Department amends 14 CCR part 214 as follows:
§ 214.12 Permit conditions.
(a) General. A mooring permit authorizes one
vessel to occupy one mooring at the location
stated on the permit. The permit may not be
transferred, sublet or used for a vessel other than
the one named on it.
(b) Maximum length. The overall length of the
permitted vessel may not exceed—
(1) In a Class A harbor, 12 meters;
(2) In a Class B harbor, 15 meters; and
(3) In a Class C harbor, 20 meters, unless the
harbor master approves a longer vessel for a
mooring at the outer edge of the field.
(c) Inspection. (1) The permit holder must
have the mooring inspected once in each
calendar year by a diver or service company
registered with the harbor office. The inspection
must—
(i) Examine the anchor, chain, shackles and
pennant along their full length;
(ii) Replace any chain whose links have lost
more than one-third of their original diameter;
and
(iii) Record the condition of each component
on the certificate form issued by the harbor
office.
(2) The permit holder must file the inspection
certificate with the harbor office by June 1 of
each year. A permit for which no certificate is
filed by that date is suspended until one is filed.
(d) Waiting list. Each harbor office maintains
one electronic waiting list for the harbor.
Applicants are offered a vacant mooring in the
order of their application date, and an applicant
who declines two offers in one season moves to
the end of the list.

Temporary Closure of the Northern
Shellfish Beds to Recreational Harvest

AGENCY: Department of Fisheries and Wildlife
(DFW).
ACTION: Temporary rule; closure.
SUMMARY: The Department is closing the
Northern Shellfish Beds to recreational harvest
of clams and oysters because routine sampling
found paralytic shellfish toxin above the action
level. The closure protects public health and will
remain in place until two consecutive weekly
samples fall below that level.
DATES: Effective 12:01 a.m. on November 6,
2026, until the Department announces the
reopening.
FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT:
Anselm Grieve, Shellfish Sanitation Program,
telephone 555–0178.
SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:
Samples taken on October 27 and November
2, 2026, at stations N–4 and N–7 contained
paralytic shellfish toxin at 112 and 146
micrograms per 100 grams of tissue. The action
level is 80 micrograms per 100 grams.
Commercial harvest in the same beds was closed
by the Department of Health on November 3,
2026.
Because the toxin poses an immediate risk to
people who eat shellfish from the beds, the
Department finds good cause to waive prior
notice and comment and to make this rule
effective less than 30 days after publication.
§ 631.40 Northern Shellfish Beds closure.
(a) Closed area. The Northern Shellfish Beds
are the intertidal and subtidal waters north of a
line from Gannet Point light to the south end of
Mire Island, including all of Tern Cove.
(b) Prohibition. While this section is in effect,
no person may—
(1) Take or possess clams or oysters from the
closed area; or
(2) Land clams or oysters taken from the
closed area at any port.
(c) Reopening. The Department will reopen
the area by notice when two consecutive weekly
samples from every station are below 80
micrograms per 100 grams.
