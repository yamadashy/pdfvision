Nurse-Led Remote Monitoring in Type 2 Diabetes

Journal of Community Health Outcomes · Vol. 14, No. 6 · June 2009

Results

Participants and primary outcome
Of 1,024 patients screened, 810 were randomised (Fig 1): 412 to remote monitoring and 398 to usual care.
Baseline characteristics were balanced between arms (Table 1); mean age was 61.3 þ 9.8 years and mean diabetes
duration 8.2 þ 5.1 years.
At 12 months, HbA1c was lower in the remote-monitoring arm (difference 20.5 percentage points, 95% CI 20.7
to 20.3; p , 0.001; Table 2). Hospital admissions fell by 52% relative to usual care (252%, p , 0.001), whereas
emergency visits were essentially unchanged (22%, p 1⁄4 0.91).

Secondary and sensitivity analyses
The effect on HbA1c was consistent across sites with .25 patients and smaller sites (interaction p 1⁄4 0.48).
Adverse events did not differ between arms (+11%, p 1⁄4 0.62); no event was judged related to the intervention.
In a per-protocol analysis restricted to patients who transmitted readings on .80% of days (n 1⁄4 351), the HbA1c
difference was 20.6 percentage points (p , 0.001).
